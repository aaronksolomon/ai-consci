## Verification and interpretation

1. State the finite domain, mathematical conventions, expected output, and verifier. Give a reproducer only when it can be shared and run safely within an explicit budget.
2. Check key claims independently of the participating agents. Distinguish a computation, a heuristic argument, a proof sketch, and a checked proof.
3. Trace one verified correction from its origin to subsequent artifacts, including failures to adopt it and any new errors introduced.
4. Separate what is observed from proposed explanations. Consider extra time, multiple sampling, human guidance, tools, and retrospective storytelling.
5. State which [claims-map](../docs/claims-and-evidence.md) entries the episode bears on. Do not infer subjective experience from successful mathematics or dialogue.

## Counterfactuals and limits

What might happen without a reviewer, a particular contribution, or shared memory? Label such claims as hypotheses unless a controlled comparison was actually run. A retrospective case study cannot recover an unobserved alternative history. Use the episode to motivate a future [pilot](../protocols/001-collaboration.md), not to replace its controls.
