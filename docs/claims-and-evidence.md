# Claims and evidence map

**Status: Draft. No project results.** Working definitions below organize inquiry; they do not settle debates about consciousness.

## Claim types and status

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Empirical | A proposition answerable through observations; identify whether proposed, externally reported, or measured here |
| Theoretical | An explanatory account and its assumptions, potentially connecting observations to a broader construct |
| Interpretive | A reading of a philosophical or religious text, or a proposed meaning for this inquiry |
| Analogical | A comparison that generates questions; resemblance alone does not establish identity |

Protocol status is separate: **Draft** means revisable and unrun; **Frozen** means committed before data collection; **Completed** means execution and deviations are documented; **Superseded** points to a replacement. **Template** contains no case evidence. **Undecided** marks an unresolved choice. These labels do not rate evidential strength.

## Four distinctions to preserve

| Construct | Working meaning | Observable handles | Limits of the inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding | For this pilot, task competence that survives checks, correction, and relevant variation | Correct answers and verifiable justification; later transfer to held-out variants | Memorization, search, tools, or evaluator cues can explain success; this operational definition does not exhaust understanding |
| Conscious access | In some theories, information becomes broadly available for flexible use and report ([S4](sources.md#s4)) | In AI, measure only functional availability: who receives an update, uses it, and changes an answer | A shared board implements communication; equating it with conscious access requires further theory and evidence |
| Subjective experience | There being something it is like for the system | No measure in Pilot 001 establishes this; reports and theory-derived indicators could inform a separate inquiry | Behavior alone here establishes neither presence nor absence; indicator interpretations depend on contested theories ([S7](sources.md#s7)) |
| Continuity | Persistence of relevant information or organization over time; distinguish this from persistence of a subject | Later: controlled memory retention, resets, provenance, and consistency across sessions | Retained text or a stable persona does not establish a continuous experiencer; loss of memory does not by itself disprove experience |

For a new entry, include scope and system boundary, source or artifact locator, what could count against it, and the strongest alternative explanation. Do not silently promote a proposed claim to a finding.
