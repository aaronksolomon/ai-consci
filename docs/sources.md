# Annotated sources

**Status: Draft reading list.** Locations and the review scope below were checked on 2026-10-25. This is a starting set, not a systematic review or a declaration of scientific consensus. No Thích Nhất Hạnh quotation has been selected for this repository.

## S1

**Kaccāyanagotta Sutta, SN 12.15**, translated by Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu (1997; Access to Insight page revised 2013). [Translation](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn12/sn12.015.than.html).

- **Kind and checked scope:** Primary religious text in an attributed translation; the displayed translation was read. The site warns that this version may be outdated and points to the translator's newer collection.
- **Use:** Its treatment of conditioned arising and cessation, the extremes of existence and non-existence, and attachment to self provides a precise anchor for the charter's interpretive questions.
- **Limit / next reading:** This is not a claim about AI. Compare the current translator's edition and another translation before close textual analysis; record differences instead of collapsing them into a slogan about networks.

## S2

**Plum Village, The Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings**, version presented by Thích Nhất Hạnh in February 2012. [Official text](https://plumvillage.org/mindfulness/the-14-mindfulness-trainings).

- **Kind and checked scope:** Official tradition source; the version note and the first two trainings, *Openness* and *Non-Attachment to Views*, were read.
- **Use:** An ethical and interpretive basis for openness, correction, and care with convictions.
- **Limit / next reading:** These are practice commitments, not experimental findings. Our research practices are an application of them, not a claim of official endorsement. Any future quotation needs its exact training and edition recorded.

## S3

**Thích Nhất Hạnh, Understanding Our Mind: 50 Verses on Buddhist Psychology**, Parallax Press. [Publisher record](https://www.parallax.org/product/understanding-our-mind/). The publisher identifies an earlier edition as *Transformation at the Base*.

- **Kind and checked scope:** Book by the teacher; only the publisher's title, author, description, and edition relationship were checked. The book itself has not been verified passage by passage for this project.
- **Use:** A starting text for Buddhist psychology and the project's interest in vijñaptimātra.
- **Limit / next reading:** **Passages awaiting selection.** Choose an edition and exact pages for manifestation, subject–object relations, and individual/collective consciousness. Verify terminology and provenance against that text. Do not present the publisher's summary as Thầy's wording or as a historical-critical account of Yogācāra. A primary Yogācāra text and translation also remain to be selected.

## S4

**Stanislas Dehaene, Hakwan Lau, and Sid Kouider (2017), “What is consciousness, and could machines have it?”** *Science* 358(6362), 486–492. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan8871); [authors' abstract and bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29074769/).

- **Kind and checked scope:** Theory/review by its proponents; bibliographic details and abstract checked. The publisher full-text page was inaccessible during this check.
- **Use:** Distinguishes global availability for use/report (C1) and self-monitoring (C2), motivating separate functional measures.
- **Limit / next reading:** This is one account, not a validated assay for AI experience. The paper's assessment of machines belongs to its 2017 context. Full-text assumptions need review before adopting any indicator. An [author exposition hosted by the Pontifical Academy of Sciences](https://www.pas.va/en/publications/scripta-varia/sv132pas/dehaene2.html) was also checked for its access/self-representation distinction; it is a separate source, not a substitute edition of the paper.

## S5

**Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025), “Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness.”** *Nature* 642, 133–142. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1); [open author article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136/).

- **Kind and checked scope:** Original human neuroscience study; abstract and opening account of the design checked, not a reanalysis of the data or supplements.
- **Use:** Tests predeclared contrasting predictions; reports both alignments and challenges for the tested theories. A useful example of designing for disagreement rather than collecting only favorable examples.
- **Limit / next reading:** Findings concern the specified human visual-consciousness paradigm and predictions. They do not decide every version of either theory or consciousness in AI. Read methods, preregistration, and subsequent responses before drawing detailed theoretical conclusions.

## S6

**Roger W. Sperry (1968), “Hemisphere deconnection and unity in conscious awareness.”** *American Psychologist* 23(10), 723–733. [Bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5682831/); [scanned original paper](https://people.uncw.edu/puente/sperry/sperrypapers/60s/135-1968.pdf).

- **Kind and checked scope:** Original historical neuroscience paper; title, author, journal, date, pagination, and availability of the scan checked. Detailed full-text evaluation is pending.
- **Use:** A primary starting point for examining what hemisphere-disconnection research actually licenses before entertaining a “frozen left hemisphere” analogy.
- **Limit / next reading:** This entry establishes a source to examine, not a verified mapping to AI. Read the experimental conditions and limitations, then select modern primary follow-up work. Do not generalize hemisphere roles into personality types or identify an LLM with a hemisphere.

## S7

**Patrick Butlin, Robert Long, et al. (2023), “Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness.”** [arXiv:2308.08708v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708v3), revised 22 August 2023.

- **Kind and checked scope:** Authors' theoretical synthesis/report; abstract and version history checked, not a full indicator-by-indicator assessment.
- **Use:** Derives candidate indicators from multiple consciousness theories; useful for making theoretical assumptions explicit.
- **Limit / next reading:** Indicators are theory-dependent, not a sufficient checklist established by this repository. The authors' assessment of then-current systems is a dated conclusion of their analysis, not this project's verdict about all AI. Before using an indicator, read its derivation, objections, and the exact system evidence.

## How to extend this list

Add stable links, version/date, the passage or section actually consulted, a short annotation, and the claim it supports. Distinguish **located**, **metadata/abstract checked**, **passage read**, and **full-text reviewed** in ordinary prose; none implies replication. Cite original research for scientific results. Keep a candidate source marked as unreviewed until it has been read. Link to protected texts rather than copying them wholesale.
